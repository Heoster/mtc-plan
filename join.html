Membership

Joining the Maples Tech Club

The club has two doors, and that is deliberate. Some selection is needed to keep project teams small enough to actually teach. But selection must never turn into a way of keeping students out of learning, so one of the two doors has no selection at all.

Two tracksThe five stagesFairness rules Staying a memberCode of conductRoles

Open Track: no selection at all

Any student of Classes VI to XII can attend the club's open workshops, awareness sessions, guest talks, exhibition days and competitions. No test, no form, no previous knowledge and no limit on numbers.

This is how most of the school will meet the club, and it is the club's main job.

Always openNo fee Classes VI–XII

Core Track: selected membership

A limited number of students are taken into the six squads each year. They get regular guidance, laboratory time for their projects, team work and the chance to represent the school outside.

In return they accept rules about attendance, conduct and teaching juniors.

Selection

The five stages of Core Track selection

Held once a year in April, with a short second intake in October for Class VI and for students newly admitted. The criteria and their weights go up on the board before the intake starts.

1

Open Call — nobody is rejected here

A notice on the school board and a simple form. The student writes their class, the squad they want, why they want to join, and anything they have already tried to make. No previous knowledge of computers is needed, and none is assumed.

Judged on: whether they want to be there
2

Aptitude screening — 30 minutes, on paper

Patterns, simple reasoning, basic mathematics, and one short written answer about a problem in the school the student would like to fix. No coding of any kind.

Weight: 25% Judged on: thinking and curiosity, not skill
3

Make something — one week

Make one small thing, chosen from a list we put up: a poster, a one-page website, a Scratch animation, a working circuit, a short video, a chart, a small program. Whatever suits the squad they applied for. Beginners' attempts are welcome and are expected.

Weight: 40% Judged on: effort and finishing it, not polish
4

Conversation — five to seven minutes

Five to seven minutes with two Core Committee members, with the Faculty Advisor sitting in. The student explains what they made, what went wrong while making it, and what they want to learn next.

Weight: 25% Judged on: honesty and willingness to listen
5

Induction and 30-day probation

Names go up on the notice board. The student signs the code of conduct along with a parent or guardian, then serves thirty days on probation, during which they have to attend regularly. Full membership is confirmed after that.

Weight: 10% attendance
Fairness

Rules that bind the selection

Access

  • No fee of any kind to apply or to be a member.
  • Previous experience is not a condition. A complete beginner who finishes a simple task will be placed above an experienced student who submits nothing.
  • Marks are not a condition either. What matters is whether a student can keep up with the club without their studies suffering.
  • Not being selected does not mean being shut out. A student who is not taken into the Core Track stays a full Open Track member and can attend every workshop the club holds.

Balance and transparency

  • At least 40% of the places go to Classes VI–IX, so the club keeps renewing itself.
  • The club will make a point of encouraging girl students to apply, and will aim for a balanced group.
  • Everything is put up in advance. Any student who is not selected will be told, if they ask, what would make their next application stronger.
  • The Faculty Advisor can overrule any selection or removal decision, and the Principal's decision is final.
Continuing

Staying a member

Attendance

Not less than 70% of sessions in the term.

Term project

One finished, written-up project each term, however small.

Conduct

Satisfactory conduct under the code of conduct below.

Removal. The Faculty Advisor may remove a member for indiscipline, for misusing school equipment or the internet, for copying, or for breaking the code of conduct. In every such case the student is heard first, and the matter is reported to the Principal.

Undertaking

Code of Conduct

Every member signs this at induction, along with a parent or guardian. The Faculty Advisor keeps the signed copy.

I, a member of the Maples Tech Club, undertake that —
1. My studies come first. I will not allow club work to affect my attendance, homework or examinations, and I accept that the Faculty Advisor may keep me out of the club at any time if my marks start falling.
2. I will respect school property. I will use the computer laboratory, its machines, the internet connection and all equipment carefully, only for club purposes, only during permitted hours, and never without a teacher present.
3. I will be honest in my work. I will not copy another person's work and present it as mine. Where I use code, images, designs or text made by somebody else, including anything produced by an artificial intelligence tool, I will say so clearly. I understand that handing in AI output as my own work counts as copying.
4. I will keep the school safe online. I will not share my school account password, will not attempt to access any account or system that is not mine, will not install unapproved software, and will not visit or share inappropriate, pirated or unlawful material.
5. I will publish nothing in the school's name without approval. No website change, notice, poster, photograph, video or social media post representing Maples Academy will be go out from me without the Faculty Advisor approving it in writing first.
6. I will protect others' privacy. I will not photograph, record or publish any student or teacher without their knowledge and consent, and I will not share anyone's personal data.
7. I will teach what I learn. I will help juniors and new members willingly, and I will not mock or discourage a beginner. Every member of this club started by knowing nothing.
8. I will conduct myself with courtesy towards teachers, staff, visitors and fellow students, in the club and when representing the school outside it.
9. I accept the authority of the Faculty Advisor and the Principal in all matters concerning the club. I understand that breaking this undertaking can mean removal from the club, and action under the school's normal disciplinary rules.
Office

Core Committee roles, elected each year in Term IV

President

Class XI–XII. Chairs meetings, owns the annual plan, reports monthly to the Faculty Advisor.

Vice-President

Deputises for the President, owns the weekly session schedule and the induction of new members.

Secretary

Keeps the attendance register, minutes and all club records; drafts correspondence for the Advisor's signature.

Technical Lead

Owns the school website, the club's repositories and the technical quality of all projects.

Design & Media Lead

Owns posters, the club's visual identity, photography and video for school events.

Outreach Lead

Handles competitions, external entries, guest speakers and communication with feeder schools.
