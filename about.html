The Club

What the Maples Tech Club actually is

A supervised workshop rather than a lecture class, and the place where the school's digital systems are looked after. This page is the club's full charter: what it is, what it is for, how it is structured, its six squads and its calendar.

01 — Definition

A workshop, not a lecture hall

The one rule that defines the Maples Tech Club is that every member ends every term holding something they have actually made. A working web page, a small program, a robot that moves, a poster, a short film, a chart, a written-up experiment. Theory is taught only as far as it is needed to make the thing. The club does not repeat the syllabus. It puts it to use.

The club also looks after the school's digital systems. Once the registrations are done, the club helps maintain the school's website, its official email accounts, its Google Classroom spaces and its event photographs, always under the faculty advisor. So the school gets something back, week after week, for the two hours it gives us.

It is non-commercial and non-political. No fee is charged to apply or to be a member, and nothing is sold.

02 — Purpose

The eight objectives

Each objective is stated with the reason it matters specifically to Maples Academy, so the club can be judged against it.

#ObjectiveWhy it matters to our school
1Close the gap between syllabus and practice CBSE now examines Artificial Intelligence and Information Technology as skill subjects. A student who has actually written and run code understands the paper far better than one who has memorised it.
2Get the school the free digital tools it qualifies for Official school email, cloud storage, Google Classroom and Microsoft Teams are available at no licence cost — but only to registered institutions. The club does the registration and the upkeep.
3Give every member a verifiable portfolio Project work hosted publicly is evidence a student can show to a college, a scholarship committee or an employer. Marks alone no longer set anyone apart.
4Build a self-sustaining peer-teaching chain Classes XI–XII train Classes VI–X. The club does not collapse when its seniors graduate, and no teacher gets landed with extra teaching.
5Represent the school externally Participation and prizes in olympiads, science fairs, hackathons and innovation challenges bring recognition to the institution and to Khatauli.
6Digitise and support school operations A maintained website, an online notice board, digital forms, event media and a results portal all done by students, supervised by staff, without paying a vendor.
7Teach digital safety and AI ethics Students already use AI and social media. Structured guidance on privacy, safe conduct online, misinformation and honesty in their work protects the school as much as it protects them.
8Widen career horizons Exposure to software, data, design and hardware careers — and to the free national learning platforms that teach them, aimed at students who would otherwise never hear of any of it.
03 — Vision & mission

What we are aiming at

Vision

No student should leave Maples Academy having only read about technology. They should leave having made something with it.

Mission — the club commits to:

  • Teach practical computing, AI literacy and electronics through hands-on projects, free of cost, to any student of the school who wishes to learn.
  • Get hold of, and look after properly, the free technology our school is eligible for, so that the benefit reaches every student and teacher, not just club members.
  • Create a peer-teaching chain in which senior members train junior members, so the club survives the departure of any individual, including its founder.
  • Represent Maples Academy in inter-school competitions, olympiads, hackathons and national innovation challenges.
  • Serve the school with real digital work: website, notices, results portal, event photography, posters and archives.
  • Promote safe, ethical and honest use of computers and artificial intelligence, including a firm stand against plagiarism and misuse.
04 — Structure

Who answers to whom

Authority flows from the Principal to the two nominated teachers, and only then to students. No student office-bearer holds financial or disciplinary authority, and no student is ever given administrator rights over a school account.

TierOfficeHeld byResponsibility
1PatronThe Principal Approves the club, the annual plan, and any participation outside the school. The final word on everything.
2Faculty AdvisorOne teacher nominated by the Principal Present at every session; countersigns all correspondence; answerable for discipline, attendance and the safety of the members.
3CoordinatorOne teacher nominated by the Principal. May be the same person as the Faculty Advisor. Holds the Google Admin Console and all administrator passwords, jointly with the Principal. The school's verified contact for Google, Microsoft, GitHub and Canva. Creates and closes accounts, and is answerable for data privacy and for which apps each class may use.
4Core Committee6 students of Classes XI–XII President, Vice-President, Secretary, Technical Lead, Design & Media Lead, Outreach Lead. Plan sessions, keep the register, report monthly to the advisor.
5Squad Leads6 students, one per domain Run the weekly agenda and mentoring for their own squad.
6Core MembersSelected students, Classes VIII–XII Attend regularly, complete term projects, mentor juniors, represent the school externally.
7Open MembersAny student, Classes VI–XII Attend open workshops and awareness sessions. No selection of any kind.

Why the club will outlive its founder

Every squad has a junior deputy. The Core Committee is elected annually in Term IV. All documentation, credentials and project files are handed to the Faculty Advisor before the outgoing batch leaves, and at least 40% of every new intake is kept for Classes VI–IX.

05 — Squads

The six domain squads

A member joins one squad but may attend any squad's open sessions.

Academic discipline — a binding rule of the club

The club stops completely for the four weeks before any school examination and for the whole of the CBSE board examination period. Coming to the club is never an excuse for falling behind in class. If a member's marks start dropping, the Faculty Advisor keeps them out until they recover. Studies come first, and that is written into the code of conduct every member signs.
