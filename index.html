The single idea behind this whole proposal

Google, Microsoft, GitHub, Canva, Figma, JetBrains and dozens of others give their software to schools and school students free. They all check eligibility the same way: through an official school email address on the school's own domain. Maples Academy already owns that domain. It is mapleskhatauli.com, and it is sitting there unused for this purpose. Verify it once with Google as a recognised school, open the Admin Console, and every door on this website opens at once. There is nothing to buy.

What the club does

Six squads. One rule: finish something.

Every member ends every term holding something they actually made — a working website, a program, a robot that moves, a poster, a short film, a data chart. Theory is taught in service of the thing being built, never instead of it.

Why it matters

Four reasons a principal should say yes

It costs the school nothing

Not one rupee. The school already owns mapleskhatauli.com, so there is no domain to buy and no yearly fee to find. Every software licence described here is free to verified schools.

The school gains real infrastructure

Official email for every teacher and student, Google Classroom, Microsoft Teams, cloud storage, a website that is actually kept up to date and an online notice board. The school keeps full administrative control of every account.

It matches CBSE and NEP 2020

CBSE now examines Artificial Intelligence and Information Technology as skill subjects, and the National Education Policy 2020 places explicit emphasis on coding, computational thinking and experiential learning. This club is the practical wing of that.

Students leave with proof, not just marks

A published project, a GitHub profile, a competition certificate — evidence a student can show to a college, a scholarship committee or an employer. Marks alone no longer distinguish anyone.

Honesty first

What this proposal does not claim

Two limits worth stating plainly

1. OpenAI's free ChatGPT for Teachers plan is United States only. It is genuinely free for verified U.S. K–12 educators, but it is not available to Indian schools today. What is available to us worldwide and free is OpenAI Academy (AI-literacy courses, including a K–12 educator track) plus the free tiers of ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot for supervised classroom use.

2. Most headline “free AI for students” offers require the student to be 18 or above and are usually aimed at college students. School students under 18 should use the free tiers, under supervision, with a teacher present.

Everything else on this site is, to the best of our research, available to an eligible Indian CBSE school right now — and every claim links to the provider's own page so the school can verify it independently.
